How it works

One particle. One loop. One unit at a time.

Most capture technology asks a plant to cool its gas, bolt on a chemical plant the size of the kiln and commit to it all at once. We start from the material instead, and design everything else around how it behaves.

01 / 03 · The particle

Carbon capture already works. The material is what fails.

Calcium-based capture is the best-suited chemistry for hot industrial gas, and it has never gone commercial because the limestone it runs on sinters shut and has to be replaced continuously. We engineer a composite particle that holds its structure instead, and supply it as a consumable.

01 · Lasts longer

More capture from less material

Built to resist the sintering and wear that make limestone crumble, so each particle keeps capturing for longer. In cycling tests to date it retains 15–20% more capture capacity. That means less sorbent bought, less purged and a lower cost per tonne captured.

02 · Reusable

A second life after capture

Spent particles are not a waste stream. Their chemistry is designed to go back into the host process as raw feed. In a cement plant they go straight into the kiln, so the sorbent itself is part of a closed loop.

03 · Built-in MRV

Verification designed into the particle

An optical tag engineered into every batch lets capture performance be read from the material itself. Our monitoring software, now in development, turns those readings into continuous, auditable capture data instead of periodic sampling.

04 · Heat as well as capture

Captures carbon, stores heat

Regenerated particles leave the calciner hot. Held in a store, they can release high-grade heat when the plant needs it, a second output alongside capture. A design feature of the system, not yet a tested claim.

Capture belongs where the heat already is

Our particle works at the temperature an industrial process already runs at, instead of asking the plant to cool its flue gas and pay for the privilege twice.

02 / 03 · The loop

Capture at the temperature the plant already runs at.

Our particles circulate between two hot vessels beside the plant. The kiln line itself is never touched.

CaptureCarbonator · ~650 °C

Flue gas meets the particles. CO₂ binds to them and the gas leaves clean, while the reaction gives off heat.

ReleaseCalciner · ~920 °C

The loaded particles are heated, let go of a pure CO₂ stream ready for storage or use, and come out regenerated.

ReturnBack to work

Regenerated particles go round again carrying useful heat. Worn ones are purged into the kiln as raw feed, so nothing is wasted.

03 / 03 · The build-out

Start with one unit. Add more as the carbon bill rises.

Instead of one enormous capture plant, the system is assembled from standard factory-built units on a shared spine. The first decision is a fraction of the size, each unit earns before the next is bought, and a plant adds capacity as free allowances phase out.

Why now

A price on carbon, and buyers for the clean alternative.

Heavy industry's emissions can't be designed away. Across our four markets, policy now puts a cost on them, and a new kind of buyer is paying for the alternative.

Unavoidable by chemistry

Around two-thirds of a cement plant's emissions come from the reaction itself. Switch every kiln to renewable power and most of them remain. Capture is the only way to deal with them.

~7–8%of global CO₂ comes from cement alone
~⅔of that is chemistry, not fuel

Policy levers in our markets

EU

Carbon priced at the border

Free emissions allowances for heavy industry are being withdrawn, and since 2026 importers pay for the carbon in cement and steel through CBAM. A proposal to bring permanent removals into the ETS is now under negotiation.

UK

A wider carbon market

A UK carbon border adjustment covering cement starts in January 2027, the UK ETS extends to energy-from-waste in 2028, and long-term government funding is backing industrial CCUS clusters.

US

Capture paid per tonne

The 45Q tax credit, retained and strengthened in 2025, pays up to $85 for each tonne captured at an industrial site and stored, and now the same for CO₂ that is put to use.

India

A compliance market for industry

India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme sets binding emission-intensity targets for cement and other heavy sectors from 2025–26. Plants that beat their target earn credits to sell; those that miss must buy them.

New demand · Data centres

AI is building fast, and its builders are buying clean materials and carbon removal.

Hyperscalers' emissions are rising with the AI build-out, and much of their footprint sits in the concrete and steel of new data centres. They have become some of the largest buyers of low-carbon cement and of durable carbon removal. These are the two markets our particle serves: cleaner cement through capture at the kiln, and permanent removals from biomass plants.
